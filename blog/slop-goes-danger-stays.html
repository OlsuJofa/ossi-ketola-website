"The slop goes, but the danger stays"

Ossi Ketola · September 2026

In July I made a call to change what goes out on Duel's streams, and I said it plainly at the time: the slop goes, but the danger stays. A few people read that as me backing off after some of the criticism the streams had drawn. That's not what happened, and I want to explain the actual difference, because I think it's a distinction most brands get wrong in one direction or the other.

"Slop" was the easy stuff — content that existed to fill airtime, not because it did anything for the brand. Cutting it cost us nothing. It wasn't earning attention, it wasn't earning trust, it was just there. Getting rid of it is basic housekeeping, not a stance.

"Danger" is different. Danger is sitting down against the best heads-up poker players alive for stakes that make tournament pros visibly uncomfortable, on stream, and letting the camera stay on when I lose $14 million in a single day. Danger is Duel's entire pitch — near-zero house edge, positioned directly against legacy casino operators who'd rather you not ask how their math works. Danger is not apologizing when the account gets banned and saying, on stream, exactly what I think happened. That's the part of the brand that actually built the audience, and it's the part I was never going to trim.

Cutting what isn't working is discipline. Cutting what built you, because it's easier, is surrender. The two get confused constantly.

The mistake most brands make under pressure is treating every piece of criticism as the same problem, and responding with the same move: soften everything, sand off whatever's left of the personality, hope the noise dies down. It usually doesn't work, because the audience that showed up for the personality notices exactly what's missing, and the critics who wanted you gone weren't going to be satisfied by half-measures anyway.

The harder version — the one I'd actually recommend — is going through the content line by line and asking which pieces are load-bearing. Most of what gets criticized isn't the thing that built you. It's noise that accumulated around the thing that built you, and you can usually cut the noise without touching the structure at all. That's what "the slop goes, but the danger stays" actually meant. It wasn't a retreat. It was maintenance.
